# A. Local proposal and environmental opening oracle

**Qualification passed.** The 305,211 tested per-proposal positive increments agree exactly in binary64 with independent full-Hamiltonian replay and arbitrary-precision integer arithmetic. Checking the whole opening sum, the largest relative error at a nonzero site was $1.25\times10^{-15}$; all reference zeros were exact. Of the tested occupied-site/proposal pairs with nonzero environmental acceptance, 63 had positive increments that naive subtraction of two binary64 incident sums lost completely. The smallest of these had numerator 5 in units of $2^{-64}$ (before the incident factor $1/3$). A too-short stencil also fails: 1,123 contributing proposals had both endpoints **at least three** lattice steps from the target. ## Native law and units

In `lattice-exploration`, `native/common/proposal.hpp::drawProposal` draws an exact uniform site and an exact uniform one of its six directions (Lemire rejection for indices, not resampling configurations). `native/cuda/ring.cuh::draw` implements the same local draw; its score rejects equal types, and its threshold check is strict `<`. The local component is not the nonlocal preparation move. The native attempted bond, counted unoriented, has probability $2/(6V)=1/(3V)$. Identical states are null moves, even if two distinct particles of one species could notionally trade labels. Swaps with a vacancy **and** between unlike occupied species count. There is no proposal conditioning on an occupied site or an eligible neighbor.

`native/common/efma.hpp::makeThresholds` reads decimal beta; `native/cuda/engine.cu` uploads these thresholds. Let $D=2^{64}$. For unlike states, write $w(d)=T(d)/D$, where $T=D$ for $d\le0$ or $\beta=0$, otherwise $T=\lfloor D\exp(-\beta d)\rfloor$. An identical-state sentinel has $T=0$. This is truncation **to zero** at sufficiently large positive barriers, not a strictly positive acceptance floor. It derives from $U<T$ for a uniform integer $U\in[0,D)$. At beta zero even $U=D-1$ is accepted for every unlike pair. At 3.2 and 3.4 the first forbidden integer barrier is 14 (inclusive). The scanner uses the shortest round-trip decimal for the ABI double, equivalent as a decimal to each archive value, not exponentiation of the binary64 beta. All 127 positive thresholds at every reported temperature agree with the actual native multiprecision routine and an independent 100-digit Decimal calculation. This qualification covers the listed decimal temperatures, not arbitrary precision near an adversarial transcendental threshold.

For occupied $x$, let $S_x=\sum_{b\ni x}w_b$ over its six incident proposals, sentinel weights zero. For nonincident $e$, define $G_{x,e}=\max(S_x(X^e)-S_x(X),0)$. The outputs are $Vh=S_x/3$ and $V^2O=\sum_{e\not\ni x}w_e G_{x,e}/9$. Both are dimensionless rescalings of per-attempt quantities. The positive part is taken **after summing over all six** target bonds, before summing over environmental proposals. It is neither a net drift, a two-step escape probability, nor a cage lifetime. Vacant targets are outside the estimand: the ABI fills their two scalars with zero, but returns their raw incident barriers and coordination just as for other sites.

## Why the stencil is complete

Write $H(X)=\sum_s I_s(n_s-k_s)^2$, where $I_s$ indicates occupancy, $n_s$ counts six occupied neighbors, and $k_s$ is 3 or 5 for species 1 or 2. For a swap $b=\{x,y\}$, only terms centered in the closed one-neighborhood $N_1(b)$ can change. Every such term depends on species/occupancies at most one more step away, so the **value** of $\Delta_b H$ depends only on $N_2(b)$. If $e$ has no endpoint there, the value is invariant. Equivalently, for a fixed $e$, changed incident bonds have an endpoint in $N_2(e)$, and their target endpoints lie in $N_3(e)$. Species-only $e$ changes no occupancy; terms change only at its two endpoints, so affected bonds have an endpoint in $N_1(e)$: away from those endpoints, their neighbor coordination and the energy changes it induces are invariant. These closed neighborhoods use actual periodic graph distance, not coordinate differences across a seam.

The implementation uses this reverse enumeration once per environmentally eligible positive-axis edge. Breadth-first integer stencils include all bonds touching $N_2(e)$ for a vacancy exchange or $N_1(e)$ for a species exchange; each distinct bond is scored once and its change added to both endpoint sums. Removing the two targets belonging to $e$ enforces nonincidence with the **target site**: an environmental swap may share the other endpoint of an incident target bond. This is why changing even eligibility (the sentinel) must be handled. The environmental bond's own new score is irrelevant to any included target. A sparse set of changed local fields is evaluated, not a full re-scan after each swap. A thread caches periodic maps and scratch arrays across parents; there is no internal parallel pool.

For the local score, write $r_s=I_s(n_s-k_s)$, $m_s=\sum_{u\sim s}r_u$, and $K_s=n_s(n_s+1)/2-I_s(k_s+1)n_s+m_s$. For unlike occupied endpoints $a,b$, the integer score is $2(k_a-k_b)(n_a-n_b)$. For occupied $a$ moving to empty neighbor $b$ it is $2[K_b-K_a-k_a n_b-(n_a+n_b-2k_a)]$. These are the native adjacent-swap formulas; alternatively expand $(r\pm1)^2-r^2$ on the nonendpoint neighbors. The evaluator caches their endpoint contributions and updates $n$, $r$, and $m$ only on the stated stencils. On our simple periodic cubes ($L\ge4$), adjacent sites have no common neighbor. The signed barrier fits int8: for a vacancy swap, the occupied site's contribution changes by at most 25 and each of the ten other neighbors by at most 9; for an occupied pair $|\Delta H|\le24$. `-128` therefore denotes equal species unambiguously, not an energy barrier or a positive barrier of zero native probability.

At beta zero $S_x$ is its number of unlike neighbors. A positive nonincident change can only occur when the state of one neighbor $y$ changes from equal to $x$ to distinct. The two endpoints of a nearest-neighbor bond cannot both be neighbors of $x$. Consequently $V^2O=(1/9)\sum_{y\sim x:\,t_y=t_x}\#\{z\sim y:t_z\ne t_y\}$. The optimized kernel uses this path count and still supplies all native integer barriers and coordination.

## Numerics, coverage and cost of the oracle check

Each integer acceptance numerator, including $2^{64}$, is stored as two base-$2^{32}$ limbs. The per-bond changes in both limbs and the sums of at most six signed changes are exact in binary64 (well below $2^{53}$). Multiplying the signed high sum by $2^{32}$ is exact; adding the low sum rounds the **exact difference**, not two previously rounded mobilities. Thus cancelling unit-acceptance moves cannot hide a smaller positive gain. Absolute zeros are preserved. The environmental products and accumulation of nonnegative terms use binary64. The independent reference multiplies and accumulates Python integers exactly with denominator $9\,2^{128}$ and converts only at the end.

Checks use fixed synthetic periodic boxes of sides 4, 5 and 6 and three actual L20 open-development parents (first open row at temperature indices 0, 4 and 9). All synthetic occupied sites in random boxes and two predetermined occupied probes per L20 anchor have every unoriented proposal replayed, including swaps entirely outside the claimed support; uniform empty and single-species boxes test no eligible proposals. For every legal tested swap, the independent C++ reference sums the entire original Hamiltonian on the trial species arrays for each queried energy difference. It shares neither stencil nor score code. All 147,966 original directed barrier entries match both the independent full-H oracle and the prior `paper_barriers.score`; total energies also match the prior independent halo Hamiltonian. Coordination is checked independently from periodic occupancy arrays.

The warmed one-thread L20 oracle, on 16 distinct open-development rows per beta, took **0.55 ms per parent at zero** and **25.2–26.4 ms per parent at nonzero beta** on the measurement host (Genoa, g++ 13.3.0). These are kernel timings with output arrays reused, not full census/shard times; they omit reducer, storage and process overhead. No new stochastic trajectory was run.

## Reproduce

Use Linux/glibc with binary128 `expf128`, a C++17 compiler, NumPy and the dependencies of the prior Python `paper_barriers`. From this repo root, set `EXPLORATION` to the canonical lattice-exploration checkout (with its vendored Boost headers unpacked at `build/include`); `STAGED` is the input directory prepared by `examples/opening_inputs.py`.

```sh
g++ -std=c++17 -O3 -march=native -fPIC -shared examples/opening_oracle.cpp -o /tmp/opening_oracle.so
g++ -std=c++17 -O3 -march=native -fPIC -shared -I"$EXPLORATION/native/common" -I"$EXPLORATION/build/include" examples/opening_oracle_reference.cpp -o /tmp/opening_oracle_reference.so
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 examples/opening_oracle_qualify.py "$STAGED" "$EXPLORATION"
```

ABI: `int opening_oracle(int L, double beta, const uint8_t *state, double *vh, double *v2o, int8_t *de, uint8_t *coord)`; zero denotes success. Contiguous `(z,y,x)` state bytes must be **unpacked** species 0, 1 or 2; `vh`, `v2o`, `coord` each have $L^3$ entries, and `de` has shape $(L^3,6)$ in $+x,-x,+y,-y,+z,-z$ order. Buffers are caller-owned and nonoverlapping. The oracle accepts sides 4–32. No compiler fast-math switches are permitted.
